================================================================================
RETENTION ENGINE — 5 REASONS VIEWERS STAY
================================================================================

A viewer decides to stay or leave every 30 seconds.

There are only 5 reasons a viewer keeps watching:

  1. UNANSWERED QUESTION — they need to know what happens
  2. REVEALED INFORMATION — they just learned something surprising
  3. EMOTIONAL INVESTMENT — they feel something (shock, rage, grief, dread)
  4. PROCEDURAL MOMENTUM — investigation is building, evidence is stacking
  5. FEAR OF MISSING OUT — "what happens next will shock you"

Every 300–350 characters (≈ 30 seconds of narration), at least ONE of
these five must activate. If none activate, the viewer leaves.

CRIME-SPECIFIC RETENTION BOOSTERS (use instead of generic triggers):
  • THE OBJECT REVEAL — a simple everyday item becomes sinister
    (muttom soup he refused / blue drum workers couldn't lift / watermelon)
  • THE BEHAVIOUR ANOMALY — a small detail that means nothing then, means
    everything now (mangalsutra left in room / wedding ring off at breakfast)
  • THE DIGITAL TRAIL — CDR / WhatsApp chats / fake FB profile / deleted
    data recovered by cyber cell — each one a new breadcrumb
  • THE FORENSIC PIVOT — DNA mismatch / fingerprint non-match / postmortem
    revealing one precise wound / FSL 54-round test — the science that ends lies
  • THE CONFESSION CRACK — pacing of interrogation, the exact moment the
    perpetrator's prepared story collapses under cross-questioning
  • THE HIDDEN MOTIVE LAYER — second motive revealed (property / VIP guest /
    insurance / foreign funding) that reframes everything the viewer thought

THE 7 STANDARD DROP-OFF DANGER ZONES
================================================================================

DANGER ZONE 1 — THE FIRST 15 SECONDS
  Problem: Opening is too slow, generic, or biographical.
  Fix: Line 1 = date + place + impossible or sinister situation.
  Test: If line 1 could open a Wikipedia article, rewrite it.
  Crime fix: Open on the body / the discovery / the last known moment.

DANGER ZONE 2 — THE 90-SECOND BIOGRAPHY SWAMP
  Problem: Too much backstory before the main action resumes.
  Fix: Backstory maximum = 4 consecutive sentences before returning
       to forward momentum. Use "लेकिन उसी दौरान..." to escape.
  Crime fix: Backstory = who the VICTIM was, not who the killer was.
       Keep killer backstory for the second half.

DANGER ZONE 3 — THE FLAT MIDDLE (minutes 4–8)
  Problem: Plot is established, viewer confidence drops.
  Fix: Minutes 4–8 must contain the story's SECOND BIGGEST surprise.
  Crime fix: The OBJECT / FORENSIC PIVOT belongs here — the one detail
       that quietly signals something is wrong before anyone else notices it.
       Example: छोटे भाई का शक — मटन सूप से मना करना (Mutton Soup Case)

DANGER ZONE 4 — THE REPETITIVE TRANSITION LOOP
  Problem: Same phrase used to signal tension 3+ times.
  Fix: No phrase used >2 times. Use Transition Phrase Bank below.

DANGER ZONE 5 — THE INVESTIGATION LECTURE
  Problem: Forensic / police scenes become abstract lists.
  Fix: Every investigation step must be shown through a physical anchor:
       CDR printout on desk / FSL lab table with test tubes / CCTV still on
       screen / fingerprint card / DNA report with red stamp / FIR form.
  Test: Can an animator draw this scene? If not, add the visual anchor.

DANGER ZONE 6 — THE VILLAIN SHRINK
  Problem: The killer is defeated too easily, making the victim's loss feel cheap.
  Fix: The killer must appear to be winning at least once after the midpoint.
  Crime fix: Show the killer's cover story working — media believing it,
       family sympathizing, police initially misled.

DANGER ZONE 7 — THE COLD LANDING
  Problem: The ending arrives without emotional preparation.
  Fix: The final 10% of the script must do 4 things in order:
       (a) Payoff the cold open's unanswered question
       (b) Name the victim — not just as a case number but as a person
       (c) State the systemic / social meaning of this crime
       (d) Leave one open question for the comments

================================================================================
THE 5 CRIME-SPECIFIC DANGER ZONES
================================================================================

CRIME DANGER ZONE C1 — THE DISAPPEARING VICTIM
  Problem: Victim is introduced, then forgotten once crime and investigation begin.
  Fix: Every 3–4 paragraphs, bring back the victim through an object they owned,
       a habit, or what they were working toward.
  Rule: Victim must be humanized before ANY gore or crime detail is introduced.

CRIME DANGER ZONE C2 — THE OBVIOUS KILLER TOO EARLY
  Problem: Viewer figures out the killer in the first 3 minutes and stops watching.
  Fix: Every crime story must have at least ONE deception layer that delays the reveal.

CRIME DANGER ZONE C3 — THE INVESTIGATION LECTURE
  Problem: Forensic / police scenes become abstract step lists.
  Fix: Each investigation step = its own dramatic scene with physical anchor + officer
       action + moment of realization + immediate consequence.

CRIME DANGER ZONE C4 — EMOTIONAL OVERLOAD
  Problem: Too many shocking reveals numb the viewer.
  Fix: After every major discovery, insert a 2–3 sentence "weight" pause.
  Rule: No more than 2 major reveals in 4 consecutive paragraphs.

CRIME DANGER ZONE C5 — THE DIGITAL EVIDENCE FLATLINE
  Problem: WhatsApp chats, CDR described as data dumps not dramatic events.
  Fix: Digital evidence presented cinematically — chat as dialogue, deletion as
       guilt act, recovery as revelation. Never list >3 pieces consecutively.

THE CRIME COLD OPEN — 5 PATTERNS (PICK ONE)
================================================================================

▶ TYPE CR-A — DISCOVERY SCENE
   A body / object / smell / sound is found. The discovery itself is the hook.

▶ TYPE CR-B — LAST KNOWN MOMENT
   The victim's last ordinary moment — before everything changed.

▶ TYPE CR-C — THE IMPOSSIBLE CLAIM
   A statement that sounds too perfect, too rehearsed, too scripted.

▶ TYPE CR-D — FORENSIC TWIST TEASE
   A science finding revealed upfront — viewer knows result, not the story.

▶ TYPE CR-E — DIGITAL DECEPTION REVEAL
   A fake identity / social media trap / digital double life as opening hook.

COLD OPEN PART B: Stakes + victim identity + case label + time/place context.
COLD OPEN PART C: Story promise + "इस कहानी की शुरुआत होती है [date] से..."

❌ NEVER OPEN WITH: Press conference / generic preamble /
   "कुछ कहानियाँ रूह तक हिला देती हैं" / killer biography before victim /
   "आज की कहानी बहुत दुखद है"

================================================================================
THE SENTENCE RHYTHM SYSTEM
================================================================================

SHORT (4–8 words) = shock, discovery, arrest, revelation
MEDIUM (9–18 words) = building tension, investigation, context
LONG (19–30 words) = atmosphere, backstory, emotional weight

Climax sequence: SHORT → MEDIUM → SHORT → SHORT → LONG (payoff exhale)
Paragraph length: 3–6 sentences. Never more than 7.

================================================================================
THE DIALOGUE SYSTEM
================================================================================

Minimum 1 line of dialogue per 1,000 characters.

Dialogue must do one of three things:
  (a) Reveal character under pressure
  (b) Deliver ultimatum or decision
  (c) Create dramatic irony (viewer knows perpetrator is lying)

CRIME-SPECIFIC DIALOGUE TYPES:
  Killer playing victim: cold, rehearsed, perfect script
  Real victim's last words/chats: innocent, scared, unaware
  Officer's realization: one quiet line that changes everything
  Family's grief: raw, incomplete, disbelief

================================================================================
THE SPECIFICITY RULE
================================================================================

Mandatory specifics: exact dates / exact times / exact amounts (₹) /
exact forensic details (54 बार FSL test) / exact counts / IPC/BNS sections /
distances / technology (CDR / DNA / CCTV / जिंक फास्फाइड)

================================================================================
THE KILLER / PERPETRATOR RULE
================================================================================

KILLER ARC: Normal life → hidden motive → planning → crime → cover story
→ appears to succeed → one overlooked detail → pivot → arrest → systemic meaning

KILLER DECEPTION TYPES:
  Type 1: Crying victim act (Priyanka — roped herself, cried on camera)
  Type 2: Cooperative informant (filed missing report himself)
  Type 3: Digital ghost (sent fake messages from victim's phone)
  Type 4: Substitute body (brought another man as "husband")
  Type 5: False backstory victim (played the trapped daughter)

================================================================================
THE EMOTIONAL ARCHITECTURE — 8 BEATS IN ORDER
================================================================================

  1. SHOCK     — Cold open. Ordinary turned sinister.
  2. CURIOSITY — Who is this? What led here?
  3. EMPATHY   — Care about the victim. Their life. Their dream.
  4. DREAD     — The plan might not be discovered. Killer is too smart.
  5. TENSION   — Investigation closing in. One wrong piece and it fails.
  6. CATHARSIS — Cover story breaks. Arrest. Confession.
  7. JUSTICE   — Named, convicted, judged. Victim acknowledged.
  8. REFLECTION — The systemic question that stays with the viewer.

DREAD must come BEFORE TENSION.
EMPATHY must be established before DREAD.
REFLECTION is NOT the CTA — it comes before CTA.

Write cold open AND ending together as a pair.
Cold open = unanswered question. Ending = complete answer + human cost.

GORE & SENSITIVITY RULES
================================================================================

Discovery: describe through the finder's reaction, not the body.

Sexual violence: acknowledge as crime, do not dramatize.
Communal/religious angle: FIR facts only. Never extrapolate to communities.
