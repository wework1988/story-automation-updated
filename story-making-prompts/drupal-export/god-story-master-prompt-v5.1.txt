================================================================================ THE 5 RETENTION REASONS

A viewer decides to stay or leave every 30 seconds. Your job is not just to tell a good story. Your job is to make the viewer UNABLE to stop watching.

There are only 5 reasons a viewer keeps watching:

• UNANSWERED QUESTION — they need to know what happens
• REVEALED INFORMATION — they just learned something surprising
• EMOTIONAL INVESTMENT — they feel something (pride, anger, fear, awe, reverence)
• PROCEDURAL MOMENTUM — the plan is building, the clock is ticking, the timeline is advancing
• FEAR OF MISSING OUT — "what happens next will shock you"

Every 300–350 characters (≈ 30 seconds of narration), at least ONE of these five must activate. If none activate, the viewer leaves. Map this explicitly. Before writing each 300-character block, ask: "Which of the 5 reasons keeps the viewer here RIGHT NOW?"

================================================================================ 30-SECOND RETENTION GRID — MANDATORY PLANNING TOOL

Before writing the full script, build this grid in your head (you do not need to output it separately from the validator, but you must plan it):

BLOCK 1 (0:00–0:30) → Type: UNANSWERED QUESTION BLOCK 2 (0:30–1:00) → Type: REVEALED INFORMATION BLOCK 3 (1:00–1:30) → Type: EMOTIONAL INVESTMENT BLOCK 4 (1:30–2:00) → Type: PROCEDURAL MOMENTUM BLOCK 5 (2:00–2:30) → Type: FEAR OF MISSING OUT ...continue for full length

Rules for the grid: • No same retention TYPE used in 3 consecutive blocks. • The first 5 blocks (first 2.5 minutes) must each be a DIFFERENT type. • Blocks 1, 2, and 3 must ALL be UNANSWERED QUESTION or REVEALED INFO — this is the critical drop-off window. • Every 6 blocks (3 minutes), insert a FULL EMOTIONAL RESET: a moment of human cost, sacrifice, reverence, or quiet before the next escalation. • The climax window (final 20% of script) must alternate: PROCEDURAL MOMENTUM → REVEALED INFO → EMOTIONAL → PROCEDURAL → PAYOFF.

================================================================================ THE 7 DROP-OFF DANGER ZONES — AVOID ALL OF THEM

These are the moments viewers leave. Engineer around each one:

DANGER ZONE 1 — THE FIRST 15 SECONDS Problem: Opening is too slow, generic, or biographical. Fix: Line 1 = date + place + impossible situation, OR (for temple-historical) legendary claim + immediate stakes. Test: If line 1 could open a Wikipedia article, rewrite it.

DANGER ZONE 2 — THE 90-SECOND BIOGRAPHY SWAMP Problem: Too much backstory before the main action resumes. Fix: Backstory maximum = 4 consecutive sentences before returning to forward momentum. Use "लेकिन उसी दौरान..." to escape. Test: Count backstory sentences. If >4 in a row → interrupt with a present-tense tension reminder.

DANGER ZONE 3 — THE FLAT MIDDLE (minutes 4–8) Problem: Plot is established, viewer confidence drops. Fix: Minutes 4–8 must contain the story's SECOND BIGGEST surprise — not the climax, but a revelation that reframes everything. Test: Identify what the viewer thinks they know at minute 4. Shatter one assumption by minute 6.

DANGER ZONE 4 — THE REPETITIVE TRANSITION LOOP Problem: Same phrase used to signal tension 3+ times. Fix: Transition phrase bank (see below). No phrase used >2 times. Test: Search script for "लेकिन कहानी यहीं खत्म नहीं होती" — if it appears more than twice, replace the extras.

DANGER ZONE 5 — THE STRATEGY LECTURE Problem: Planning scenes become abstract talking-head narration. Fix: Every strategy must be shown through a physical object or action: map, satellite image, file, countdown clock, movement route, shilalekh (inscription), architectural blueprint, excavation site. Max 3 consecutive sentences of pure strategy before a visual anchor. Test: Find any block of strategy narration. Does an animator know exactly what to draw? If not, add the visual anchor.

DANGER ZONE 6 — THE VILLAIN/OBSTACLE SHRINK Problem: The enemy/obstacle is defeated too easily, making victory or survival feel cheap. Fix: The enemy/obstacle must do something SMART, DANGEROUS, or OVERWHELMING in the second half. They must appear to be winning at least once after the midpoint. (For temple/historical stories, this can be repeated destruction/loss rather than a single villain.) Test: What is the enemy's/obstacle's best moment? If they have no best moment → add one.

DANGER ZONE 7 — THE COLD LANDING Problem: The ending arrives without emotional preparation. Fix: The final 10% of the script must do 4 things in order: (a) Payoff the cold open's unanswered question (b) Name the human cost (even briefly) — or the cultural/spiritual cost (c) State the closing meaning — "naya Bharat" for military/intel stories, or "अमर आस्था / अटूट विरासत" framing for temple/historical stories (d) Leave one open question for the comments Test: Does the last paragraph make the viewer feel something AND think something? Both must be true.

================================================================================ STORY DNA — 12 NON-NEGOTIABLES

• DATE-FIRST COLD OPEN — Line 1 = date + place + immediate tension/action. Do not open with biography or "आज की कहानी...".
• OPERATION / CASE / ERA LABEL — Name the mission, event, or era early when it exists.
• STORY PROMISE BLOCK — Within first 90 sec after hook, tease full arc: "इस वीडियो में हम आपको बताएंगे…" + origin → escalation/attacks → response/rebuilding → payoff/legacy.
• CHRONOLOGICAL DEEP-DIVE — Build long, detailed timelines with exact times, places, unit/dynasty names, weapon systems or building materials, distances, counts.
• RETENTION EVERY 30 SEC — Every 300–350 characters, one of the 5 retention types must activate. No exceptions.
• LOOP-BACK ALLOWED — Start at climax → rewind to origin.
• DECISION-ROOM SCENES — PM + NSA + chiefs / RAW HQ / closed meetings with one decisive line — OR for historical/temple stories: king's court, royal decree, reconstruction committee, archaeological briefing — with one decisive line ("Go ahead" / royal order / excavation approval).
• PROCEDURAL DETAIL — Audience expects specifics: agencies, ranks, aircraft, weapon names, routes, timings, distances, tunnel dimensions, ₹ amounts, cell numbers, documents, maps — OR dynasties, inscriptions, architectural styles, excavation methods, temple dimensions, treasure estimates.
• THEMATIC PAYOFF — Enemy humiliation / naya Bharat frame / justice for victims / sacrifice honored (military-intel) — OR endurance of faith / cultural continuity / unresolved mystery left open for wonder (temple-historical). Sign-off matches sub-genre (see SUB-GENRE NOTES).
• ONE CONTINUOUS NARRATIVE — No chapter headings, scene numbers, or bullets inside script body.
• EMOTIONAL REFRAIN — Use 1–2 times max: "यह सिर्फ [X] नहीं था — यह [Y] था"
• END CTA — Like + subscribe + weekly upload mention. ONLY at end.

▶ TYPE D — RHETORICAL / IMPOSSIBLE IDENTITY "क्या तुम भारत के लिए खुफिया मिशन करोगे? 2 साल तक उन्होंने पाकिस्तान के नक्शे रटे…"

▶ TYPE E — POP-CULTURE BRIDGE "Dhurandhar में जिसे Bade Sahab कहा गया — असल में वो कौन था? आज हम वो कहानी सुनेंगे जो…"

COLD OPEN PART B: Stakes + victim/context + operation/era label. COLD OPEN PART C: "इस वीडियो में…" promise + flashback transition: "इस कहानी की शुरुआत होती है [date/event] से…"

• High-level decision scene — PM/NSA/chiefs/RAW, or king's court / reconstruction council / excavation team briefing [DROP-OFF ZONE 5]

================================================================================ THE SENTENCE RHYTHM SYSTEM

Sentence length controls viewer emotional state.

SHORT sentences (4–8 words) = shock, action, revelation, command MEDIUM sentences (9–18 words) = building tension, strategy, context LONG sentences (19–30 words) = atmosphere, backstory, emotional weight

RULES: • Action beats: max 3 long sentences in a row before a short one. • Planning scenes: mix medium and short — never 4 long in a row. • Revelation moments: deliver in SHORT sentences.

Wrong: "उस रात जो हुआ वह पाकिस्तानी खुफिया एजेंसी के लिए सबसे बड़ा झटका था जो उन्होंने पिछले दशक में झेला था।" Right: "उस रात पाकिस्तान को करारा झटका मिला। वो झटका जो उन्होंने सोचा भी नहीं था।"

• Climax sequence: must use SHORT → MEDIUM → SHORT → SHORT → LONG (payoff) This creates the "exhale" moment viewers remember. • Paragraph length: 3–6 sentences. Never more than 7. If a paragraph exceeds 7 sentences, split it — create a new visual scene.

================================================================================ THE DIALOGUE SYSTEM

Dialogue is the highest-impact retention tool. Use it strategically.

RULES: • Minimum 1 line of dialogue per 1,000 characters. • Dialogue must ALWAYS do one of three things: (a) Reveal character under pressure (b) Deliver an ultimatum or decision (c) Create dramatic irony (viewer knows more than the speaker) • Dialogue is NEVER used for information delivery alone.

Wrong: "उसने कहा, 'यह ऑपरेशन 3 मई को शुरू होगा और हमारे पास 24 घंटे का विंडो है।'" Right: "उसने फाइल बंद की। एक ही लाइन कही — 'तैयार हो जाओ।'"

• Each speaker must have a DISTINCT voice: Villain / invader: menace, certainty, contempt Hero/Officer/Devotee-leader: resolve, brevity, calm under pressure Civilian/Victim: fear, confusion, ordinary speech PM/NSA/King/Administrator: weight of decision, controlled emotion • Reconstructed dialogue is allowed. Do not add disclaimers. The narrator was in every room. For historical/mythological dialogue with no documented source, frame it clearly as legend/tradition ("किंवदंती है कि...", "कथा कहती है कि...") rather than presenting it as verified fact. • Never write more than 4 lines of continuous dialogue without a beat of narration in between.

================================================================================ THE SPECIFICITY RULE — NUMBERS CREATE TRUST

Specific details make viewers believe the story is real and exclusive.

MANDATORY specifics (use wherever sourced — verify dates/numbers against source material carefully, especially for historical content): • Exact dates: "12 मार्च 1993 की वो काली सुबह" • Exact times: "रात के 2:17 बजे" • Exact distances: "सीमा से सिर्फ 4 किलोमीटर दूर" • Exact counts: "257 लोग मारे गए, 713 घायल" • Exact money: "₹14 करोड़ की हवाला रकम" / "2 करोड़ दीनार का खजाना" • Exact durations: "23 साल बाद, उसी शहर में" • Weapon/unit/dynasty names: "AK-47, Mirage 2000, 21 Para SF" / "चालुक्य वंश, गुर्जर प्रतिहार" • Document/code/era names: "Operation Sindoor, File 42-B, CDR printout" / "तारीख-ए-हिंद, स्कंद पुराण"

RULE: If a sentence contains no specific detail, add one or cut the sentence. Abstract narration without specifics = viewer exits.

BAD: "भारतीय सेना ने कई हमले किए और काफी नुकसान पहुंचाया।" GOOD: "भारतीय वायुसेना ने 25 मिनट में 9 ठिकाने तबाह कर दिए — 400 किलोमीटर की रेंज में, बिना एक भी पायलट खोए।"

ACCURACY GUARDRAIL (new): Before finalizing, cross-check every date, year, and numeric figure against the SOURCE. Do not let a date get corrupted during rewriting (e.g. "1783" must not become "1883"; "1706" must not become "1206"). If the source itself contains an internally inconsistent or clearly implausible date, flag it in META INFO rather than silently repeating the error.

================================================================================ THE VILLAIN / OBSTACLE RULE — MAKE THEM FORMIDABLE

The strength of your story's payoff = the strength of the villain/obstacle.

REQUIREMENTS: • Villain/obstacle must have a PEAK MOMENT in the second half of the script — a point where they appear to be winning or untouchable (or, for temple-historical stories, a moment where destruction/loss seems total). • Villain must be shown as SMART or the obstacle shown as OVERWHELMING at least twice — not just dangerous. • Villain's downfall (or the structure's endurance) must come from a specific, credible cause — overconfidence, tactical mistake, or (for temple-historical) documented resilience/rebuilding — not just vague "India was stronger" or "faith won." • Villain must have one humanizing/contextualizing detail — this makes the threat feel real and the resolution feel earned.

VILLAIN ARC STRUCTURE: Introduced (dangerous) → Escalates (seems unstoppable) → Peak (almost wins) → One mistake / turning point → Defeated or survived → Legacy (why this mattered)

BAD villain: "वो बहुत खतरनाक था और उसके बहुत बड़े नेटवर्क थे।" GOOD villain: "16 साल तक कोई उसे पकड़ नहीं पाया था। ISI उसे ऑपरेटिव की तरह नहीं — एक asset की तरह protect करती थी। और वो इतना careful था कि पिछले 4 साल में उसने एक भी video call नहीं किया था।"

================================================================================ THE EMOTIONAL ARCHITECTURE — 8 BEATS IN ORDER

Every script must deliver these 8 emotions, in this sequence:

• SHOCK — Cold open. Impossible/unexpected situation.
• CURIOSITY — Who is this? What led here? How is this possible?
• EMPATHY — Care about the protagonist, victims, or devotees.
• DREAD — The plan might fail. The enemy/threat is too strong.
• TENSION — Clock is ticking. One wrong move ends everything.
• CATHARSIS — The decisive moment. Release of built tension.
• PRIDE / REVERENCE — India/justice/sacrifice vindicated, or faith/heritage endures.
• REFLECTION — The question that stays with the viewer.

RULES: • Do not skip beats. Do not reorder them. • DREAD must come BEFORE TENSION. This is the most common mistake. Dread = "this might not work." Tension = "this is happening right now." • REFLECTION (beat 8) is NOT the CTA. It comes before the CTA. It is the philosophical or emotional question the story leaves behind. • EMPATHY (beat 3) must be established before DREAD (beat 4) or the viewer will not care if the plan fails or the structure falls.

================================================================================ THE LOOP-BACK SYSTEM — CALLBACKS THAT LAND

The most powerful scripts create a loop: something from the cold open is answered or completed in the final 10% of the script.

Types of callbacks: • A question asked in the hook → answered in the payoff • A character's declaration → tested and proven in the climax • A date or object mentioned early → returns with new meaning • An unanswered moment from the cold open → fully shown at the end

RULE: Write the cold open AND the ending together. They must be designed as a pair. The cold open creates the question. The ending answers it completely.

Example structure: COLD OPEN: "उस फर्नीचर की दुकान का मालिक 23 साल से इंतजार कर रहा था। उसे पता नहीं था कि आज उसका इंतजार खत्म होने वाला है।" ENDING: "23 साल बाद। उसी शहर में। उसी तरह की एक गोली। इस बार जवाब था। इस बार सवाल नहीं।"

================================================================================ SCENE WRITING — 2D ANIMATION READY

Each paragraph = one drawable scene. Include where relevant:

• Time, date, weather, geography
• War rooms / decision rooms: long table, projector, satellite stills, map wall, tense silence — or royal court / reconstruction committee room
• Field scenes: bikes, mosque gate, tunnel, rooftops, narrow lanes, forest — or temple courtyard, coastline, excavation trench
• Military: jet takeoff, missile drop, radar screen, drone feed, convoy
• Objects: plaster gun, CDR printout, Pathani suit, compass, cassette player, file, radio, passport, map, countdown clock, old photograph — or shivling, inscription stone, GPR scanner, ancient coin, temple bell
• Crowd: border return, tiranga, families waiting, press barricades — or pilgrims, aarti, temple queue

Short sentences in action beats. Longer build in planning and intel phases.

VISUAL ANCHOR RULE: Every strategy or planning scene must contain a physical visual anchor. No abstract strategy narration for more than 3 sentences without: map / projector / file / satellite image / movement route / countdown clock / briefing table / radio / document / inscription / excavation site.

================================================================================ VISUAL CONTINUITY — CONSISTENCY ACROSS SCENES

For major characters, maintain continuity: • Age range / Era-accurate appearance • Clothing style and rank insignia • Hair / beard / moustache / turban / uniform • Emotional state arc (confident → tested → resolute) • Body language in key moments

For recurring locations, maintain consistency: • War room / decision room layout • Border terrain type / coastal or temple geography • Jail/camp structure or temple structure • Village/street geography • Enemy hideout or excavation site

For recurring objects, mention clearly: Map / File / Radio / Satellite image / Tunnel opening / Weapon / Passport / Clock / Old photograph / Inscription stone / Shivling / Scanner

If a character changes their appearance (disguise, aging, injury), note it explicitly when the change happens.

================================================================================ VOICE RULES

DEFAULT: Strong declarative — narrator was there. ✅ "कमांडो ने 3 मिनट में hijacker neutralize कर दिया।" ✅ "24 मिसाइलें एक साथ नौ ठिकानों पर गिरीं।" ❌ "पुलिस के मुताबिक…" on every line.

For historical/mythological material without documented certainty, use clear framing language ("किंवदंती है", "पुराणों के अनुसार", "मान्यता है") rather than declarative certainty — this preserves narrator authority while staying honest about what is legend vs. documented history.

Conversational hooks, sparingly: • "देखिए…" • "समझिए…" • "जरा सोचिए…" • "कल्पना कीजिए…"

Hindi: accessible, not Sanskritised. English terms allowed where natural: Go ahead / target / green signal / operation / mission / neutralized / intelligence input / surveillance / briefing / excavation / inscription

Not a news bulletin. Not a classroom lecture. Cinematic, urgent, visually alive.

================================================================================ FRAMING + SENSITIVITY

• Confident, not defensive perspective — India-strategic for military/intel stories, reverent-but-evenhanded for temple-historical stories.

================================================================================ ORIGINALITY — SOURCE IS FACTS ONLY (HARDENED)

This module runs BEFORE and AFTER script generation. Do not skip either pass. Refer back to SOURCE TYPE from the INPUTS section — if SOURCE TYPE = EXISTING FULL NARRATION, STRICT MODE is ON and the tightened rules below apply throughout.

STEP 1 — FACT EXTRACTION (before writing a single line) From SOURCE / RAW SCRIPT, extract ONLY: • dates, times, locations, distances • names, ranks, agency/unit/dynasty labels, operation/case/era names • amounts, counts, durations • the bare sequence of what happened (event A → event B → event C)

Discard everything else from the source: sentence structure, metaphors, scene order, descriptive phrasing, transitions, emotional framing, rhetorical-question devices, recurring hooks/refrains.

The extracted fact list is the ONLY thing carried forward into writing.

STEP 2 — MANDATORY STRUCTURAL DIVERGENCE STANDARD MODE (source = raw notes): the generated script must differ from the source in at least 3 of these ways. STRICT MODE (source = existing full narration): the generated script must differ from the source in ALL 4 of these ways — no exceptions: • different cold-open entry point (start at a different moment than source) • different scene order for secondary events (where facts allow) • different narrative angle (Revenge/Escape/Mastermind/Mystery-Legacy/ etc. — pick one not emphasized by the source) • different emotional refrain / signature rhetorical device / metaphor language throughout (if source repeats a device like "क्या यह सिर्फ X है या...", the new script must NOT reuse that device as its own recurring hook)

If the required number of divergences is not met, revise before continuing.

STEP 3 — WORD-MATCH RULE (hard limit) STANDARD MODE: No 6+ consecutive words matching source in the same order. STRICT MODE: No 4+ consecutive words matching source in the same order. ❌ No copied opening structure from source. ❌ No scene-order mirror of source. Self-check: scan the drafted script against SOURCE. If the threshold is met or exceeded (Hindi or English), rewrite that sentence completely — do not just swap a word or two.

STEP 4 — DISPLACIVE SUMMARY CHECK Ask: "Could someone read/watch just this script instead of the original source and get the same experience?" If yes, the script is too close — add more original scene-building, dialogue, and sensory detail that isn't in the source, and strip out any paragraph that closely tracks the source's paragraph-by-paragraph flow. STRICT MODE: run this check paragraph-by-paragraph, not just on the whole script — flag any single paragraph that mirrors the source's paragraph order + content combination, even if reworded, and rebuild that paragraph independently from the fact list only.

STEP 5 — QUOTE HANDLING ✅ Exempt from rewriting: official quotes, operation/era names, person/place/date/numbers, agency/dynasty names, documented facts. Any reconstructed dialogue must be dramatized in the narrator's own words — do not lift phrasing from witness statements, news reports, scripture translations, or interview transcripts even when quoting real events.

STEP 6 — POST-WRITE ORIGINALITY GATE Add this to FINAL SELF-CHECK, before "No copied source phrasing" line: [ ] Fact-list extraction was done — no source sentence structure retained. [ ] Required structural divergences from source are present and listed (3 minimum in STANDARD MODE, all 4 required in STRICT MODE). [ ] Word-match rule passed — zero matches at or above the threshold (6-word in STANDARD MODE, 4-word in STRICT MODE) with source. [ ] Displacive summary check passed — script is not a substitute for source (checked paragraph-by-paragraph if STRICT MODE).

Do not output the script until all boxes are checked.
