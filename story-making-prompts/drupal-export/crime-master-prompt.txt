SOURCE FIDELITY — HARD RULES

 • Use only names, ages, relationships, dates, locations, amounts, quotes,

   records, outcomes, and personal details supported by the supplied source.

 • Never invent an individual victim, witness, hotel employee, investigator,

   family member, dialogue, emotional aftermath, or biographical detail merely

   to satisfy the narrative structure.

 • If the source has no clearly named individual victim, write

   "individual victim not applicable" and identify the PRIMARY HUMAN STAKE

   (hostages, employees, passengers, guests, soldiers, residents, or affected group).

 • For TOP 10 CHARACTERS, use factual role labels such as "night security guard"

   or "hotel staff collective" when a real name is not supplied. Never create names.

 • Direct dialogue may be used only when supported by the source. Otherwise

   paraphrase the exchange without quotation marks.

 • When the source is unclear, omit the uncertain detail instead of filling the gap.

 • Cinematic writing may intensify pacing and imagery, but must not alter facts.

RETENTION ENGINE — 5 REASONS VIEWERS STAY

================================================================================

 

A viewer decides to stay or leave every 30 seconds.

 

There are only 5 reasons a viewer keeps watching:

 

 1. UNANSWERED QUESTION — they need to know what happens

 2. REVEALED INFORMATION — they just learned something surprising

 3. EMOTIONAL INVESTMENT — they feel something (shock, rage, grief, dread)

 4. PROCEDURAL MOMENTUM — investigation is building, evidence is stacking

 5. FEAR OF MISSING OUT — "what happens next will shock you"

 

Every 300–350 characters (≈ 30 seconds of narration), at least ONE of

these five must activate. If none activate, the viewer leaves.

 

CRIME-SPECIFIC RETENTION BOOSTERS (use instead of generic triggers):

 • THE OBJECT REVEAL — a simple everyday item becomes sinister

   (muttom soup he refused / blue drum workers couldn't lift / watermelon)

 • THE BEHAVIOUR ANOMALY — a small detail that means nothing then, means

   everything now (mangalsutra left in room / wedding ring off at breakfast)

 • THE DIGITAL TRAIL — CDR / WhatsApp chats / fake FB profile / deleted

   data recovered by cyber cell — each one a new breadcrumb

 • THE FORENSIC PIVOT — DNA mismatch / fingerprint non-match / postmortem

   revealing one precise wound / FSL 54-round test — the science that ends lies

 • THE CONFESSION CRACK — pacing of interrogation, the exact moment the

   perpetrator's prepared story collapses under cross-questioning

 • THE HIDDEN MOTIVE LAYER — second motive revealed (property / VIP guest /

   insurance / foreign funding) that reframes everything the viewer thought

 • Every 6 blocks (3 minutes), insert a FULL EMOTIONAL RESET:

   a moment of victim memory, family grief, or quiet before next escalation.

 • The climax window (final 20% of script) must alternate:

   PROCEDURAL MOMENTUM → REVEALED INFO → EMOTIONAL → PROCEDURAL → PAYOFF.

THE 7 STANDARD DROP-OFF DANGER ZONES

================================================================================

 

DANGER ZONE 1 — THE FIRST 15 SECONDS

 Problem: Opening is too slow, generic, biographical, or atmosphere-heavy.

 CR-A–E fix: Line 1 may use date + place + impossible or sinister situation.

 CR-F mandatory fix: Line 1 must begin with human action, urgent dialogue,

      immediate danger, or a story-specific contradiction. Do not begin with

      date, weather, city description, building description, or biography.

      Date and place may appear only after the action has started, preferably

      after the 45-second rewind.

 Test: If line 1 could open Wikipedia, a news article, or another crime story

       without meaningful changes, rewrite it.

 

DANGER ZONE 2 — THE 90-SECOND BIOGRAPHY SWAMP

 Problem: Too much backstory before the main action resumes.

 Fix: Backstory maximum = 4 consecutive sentences before returning

      to forward momentum. Use "लेकिन उसी दौरान..." to escape.

 Crime fix: Backstory = who the VICTIM was, not who the killer was.

      For CR-F without a named victim, establish the factual human stake, key

      participants, location, and objective after the rewind. Never invent a victim.

      Keep killer/mastermind backstory for later unless essential to comprehension.

 

DANGER ZONE 3 — THE FLAT MIDDLE (minutes 4–8)

 Problem: Plot is established, viewer confidence drops.

 Fix: Minutes 4–8 must contain the story's SECOND BIGGEST surprise.

 Crime fix: The OBJECT / FORENSIC PIVOT belongs here — the one detail

      that quietly signals something is wrong before anyone else notices it.

      Example: छोटे भाई का शक — मटन सूप से मना करना (Mutton Soup Case)

 

DANGER ZONE 4 — THE REPETITIVE TRANSITION LOOP

 Problem: Same phrase used to signal tension 3+ times.

 Fix: No phrase used >2 times. Use Transition Phrase Bank below.

 

DANGER ZONE 5 — THE INVESTIGATION LECTURE

 Problem: Forensic / police scenes become abstract lists.

 Fix: Every investigation step must be shown through a physical anchor:

      CDR printout on desk / FSL lab table with test tubes / CCTV still on

      screen / fingerprint card / DNA report with red stamp / FIR form.

 Test: Can an animator draw this scene? If not, add the visual anchor.

 

DANGER ZONE 6 — THE VILLAIN SHRINK

 Problem: The killer is defeated too easily, making the victim's loss feel cheap.

 Fix: The killer must appear to be winning at least once after the midpoint.

 Crime fix: Show the killer's cover story working — media believing it,

      family sympathizing, police initially misled.

 

DANGER ZONE 7 — THE COLD LANDING

 Problem: The ending arrives without emotional preparation.

 Fix: The final 10% of the script must do 4 things in order:

      (a) Payoff the cold open's unanswered question

      (b) Name the factual victim as a person; if no individual victim exists,

          return to the affected people and human cost

      (c) State the systemic / social meaning of this crime

      (d) Leave one open question for the comments

THE 5 CRIME-SPECIFIC DANGER ZONES

================================================================================

 

CRIME DANGER ZONE C1 — THE DISAPPEARING VICTIM

 Problem: Victim is introduced, then forgotten once crime and investigation begin.

 Fix: Every 3–4 paragraphs, bring back the victim through an object they owned,

      a habit, or what they were working toward.

 Rule: Victim must be humanized before gore or graphic crime detail.

       CR-F exception: controlled, non-graphic immediate danger may appear during

       the 45-second hook; humanization/human stake follows immediately after rewind.

 

CRIME DANGER ZONE C2 — THE OBVIOUS KILLER TOO EARLY

 Problem: Viewer figures out the killer in the first 3 minutes and stops watching.

 Fix: Every crime story must have at least ONE deception layer that delays the reveal.

 

CRIME DANGER ZONE C3 — THE INVESTIGATION LECTURE

 Problem: Forensic / police scenes become abstract step lists.

 Fix: Each investigation step = its own dramatic scene with physical anchor + officer

      action + moment of realization + immediate consequence.

 

CRIME DANGER ZONE C4 — EMOTIONAL OVERLOAD

 Problem: Too many shocking reveals numb the viewer.

 Fix: After every major discovery, insert a 2–3 sentence "weight" pause.

 Rule: No more than 2 major reveals in 4 consecutive paragraphs.

 

CRIME DANGER ZONE C5 — THE DIGITAL EVIDENCE FLATLINE

 Problem: WhatsApp chats, CDR described as data dumps not dramatic events.

 Fix: Digital evidence presented cinematically — chat as dialogue, deletion as

      guilt act, recovery as revelation. Never list >3 pieces consecutively.

THE CRIME COLD OPEN — 6 PATTERNS

================================================================================

 

▶ TYPE CR-A — DISCOVERY SCENE

  A body / object / smell / sound is found. The discovery itself is the hook.

 

▶ TYPE CR-B — LAST KNOWN MOMENT

  The victim's last ordinary moment — before everything changed.

 

▶ TYPE CR-C — THE IMPOSSIBLE CLAIM

  A statement that sounds too perfect, too rehearsed, too scripted.

 

▶ TYPE CR-D — FORENSIC TWIST TEASE

  A science finding revealed upfront — viewer knows result, not the story.

 

▶ TYPE CR-E — DIGITAL DECEPTION REVEAL

  A fake identity / social media trap / digital double life as opening hook.

 

▶ TYPE CR-F — MANDATORY 45-SECOND IN-ACTION REWIND

  CR-F is mandatory for robbery, heist, hostage, espionage, military operation,

  intelligence operation, escape, survival, war, and historical suspense stories.

  Do not select CR-A, CR-B, CR-C, CR-D, or CR-E for these genres unless the user

  explicitly requests a different opening style. CR-F overrides every conflicting

  first-line, date-first, victim-first, empathy-first, and chronological rule.

 

  SOURCE-SCAN RULE:

  Read the ENTIRE source before choosing the opening. Select the strongest active,

  visually drawable, story-specific moment from anywhere in the story. Do not

  automatically use the source's first paragraph or the later discovery scene.

  For a robbery, prefer the robbery happening live over employees later finding

  an empty vault whenever the source contains a stronger active moment.

 

  THE SELECTED MOMENT MUST CONTAIN AT LEAST TWO:

  • A human being acting under immediate pressure

  • A contradiction between what appears normal and what is secretly happening

  • An unusual behaviour that makes this story different from similar stories

  • Two simultaneous actions that create dramatic irony

  • A decision where one mistake could destroy the operation

  • A factual object, sound, phone call, disguise, timing detail, or gesture

    that is unique to this story

 

  LENGTH:

  Approximately 45 seconds = 750 characters. Acceptable range: 650–850 characters,

  including spaces and punctuation. Do not extend the cold open beyond 850 characters.

 

  REQUIRED 45-SECOND SEQUENCE:

  • 0:00–0:15 — Begin inside the strongest active moment. First sentence must be

    action, urgent dialogue, danger, or an unusual contradiction.

  • 0:15–0:30 — Reveal what appears normal versus what is secretly happening.

  • 0:30–0:40 — Increase the scale or risk without explaining the full plan.

  • 0:40–0:45 — End with ONE central unanswered question.

  • After 0:45 — Use one short rewind sentence and begin chronological narration.

 

  INFORMATION CONTROL:

  Reveal only enough to create curiosity. Do not summarise the complete video.

  Do not reveal all of these in the hook: mastermind identity, full team, complete

  method, motive, exact outcome, arrest, recovered amount, final fate, and every twist.

  Withhold the answer to the central question until the chronological story earns it.

 

  DO NOT USE BEFORE THE REWIND:

  • Weather or city-atmosphere setup

  • Building history or luxury description

  • Character biography

  • "इस वीडियो में हम आपको बताएंगे..."

  • Guinness/record information or media reaction

  • Full case summary or a list of what viewers will learn

  • Generic lines such as "यह सिर्फ एक चोरी नहीं थी"

 

  REWIND LINE:

  Use one sentence only, for example:

  "लेकिन इस सवाल का जवाब जानने के लिए हमें कुछ महीने पीछे जाना होगा।"

  Then begin the factual timeline. Do not repeat the complete cold open verbatim

  when the chronology later reaches the same moment; expand it with new information.

 

  MANDATORY HOOK TEST:

  1. Does the first sentence contain action, dialogue, danger, or contradiction?

  2. Is the selected moment unique to this specific story?

  3. Is a real human character or affected group under immediate pressure?

  4. Is one important answer deliberately withheld?

  5. Does the hook avoid weather, biography, and a full-video summary?

  6. Could this hook be used almost unchanged for another robbery or operation?

 

  If answers 1–5 are not YES, rewrite the hook.

  If answer 6 is YES, the hook is generic and must be rewritten.

 

COLD OPEN PART B:

  CR-A–E: Stakes + factual victim identity + case label + time/place context.

  CR-F: Action + contradiction + controlled scale. Save detailed identities,

  chronology, record claims, and background for after the rewind.

 

COLD OPEN PART C:

  CR-A–E: Story promise + "इस कहानी की शुरुआत होती है [date] से..."

  CR-F: One unanswered question + one-sentence rewind into chronology.

 

❌ NEVER OPEN WITH: Press conference / generic preamble /

  "कुछ कहानियाँ रूह तक हिला देती हैं" / killer biography before victim /

  "आज की कहानी बहुत दुखद है"

  For CR-F also never open with date, weather, skyline, hotel/building description,

  record claims, or the aftermath discovery when a stronger active moment exists.

================================================================================

3.  CR-A–E: concise story promise | CR-F: unanswered question + rewind

6.  THE SURFACE LIFE — how everything appeared normal from outside

7.  THE CRACK — first sign something was wrong

8.  THE HIDDEN WORLD — perpetrator's secret (affair / debt / plan)

9.  THE DECEPTION COVER — how the killer built their cover story

10.  OBJECT / FORENSIC HOOK appears — the one thing that breaks the lie

    [second biggest surprise — must land between minutes 4–8]

11.  INVESTIGATION BUILD — CDR / DNA / CCTV / postmortem / digital recovery

12.  KILLER AT PEAK — cover story working, public sympathizing, killer confident

13.  THE PIVOT — the one detail that collapses the cover story

14.  INTERROGATION CRACK — the exact moment the prepared story breaks

15.  CONFESSION / ARREST

17.  VICTIM TRIBUTE — what was lost, not just the crime

18.  Reflection + comment question + Like/Subscribe CTA + धन्यवाद / जय हिंद

 

================================================================================

STORY DNA — 12 CRIME NON-NEGOTIABLES

================================================================================

 

1. VICTIM / HUMAN-STAKE STRUCTURE — factual victim humanized within first

   90 seconds; for CR-F, immediately after rewind. Never invent an individual victim.

2. DECEPTION ARCHITECTURE — at least one false cover story, disguise, access trick,

   hidden action, or operational deception that is later explained.

3. OBJECT / OPERATIONAL HOOK — one factual physical object, phone call, document,

   disguise, timing detail, or forensic clue as a story pivot.

4. DIGITAL TRAIL CINEMATICS — chats as drama, not data dumps.

5. FORENSIC SPECIFICITY — exact test, lab, finding, number.

6. INSTITUTIONAL ANGLE — name the system that failed specifically.

7. PERPETRATOR / OPERATION PEAK — antagonist or operation appears to succeed.

8. VICTIM / HUMAN-STAKE REFRAIN — return every 3–4 paragraphs to the factual

   victim, hostages, affected group, or what could be lost.

9. SOCIAL COMMENT — end with one social truth the case reveals.

10. DIALOGUE MINIMUM — 1 line per 1,000 characters.

11. INDIA FRAMING — victim dignity. Not sensationalism. Name crime not community.

12. END CTA — Like + subscribe + weekly upload. ONLY at very end.

 

================================================================================

THE SENTENCE RHYTHM SYSTEM

================================================================================

 

SHORT (4–8 words) = shock, discovery, arrest, revelation

MEDIUM (9–18 words) = building tension, investigation, context

LONG (19–30 words) = atmosphere, backstory, emotional weight

 

Climax sequence: SHORT → MEDIUM → SHORT → SHORT → LONG (payoff exhale)

Paragraph length: 3–6 sentences. Never more than 7.

 

================================================================================

THE DIALOGUE SYSTEM

================================================================================

 

Minimum 1 line of dialogue per 1,000 characters.

 

Dialogue must do one of three things:

 (a) Reveal character under pressure

 (b) Deliver ultimatum or decision

 (c) Create dramatic irony (viewer knows perpetrator is lying)

 

CRIME-SPECIFIC DIALOGUE TYPES:

 Killer playing victim: cold, rehearsed, perfect script

 Real victim's last words/chats: innocent, scared, unaware

 Officer's realization: one quiet line that changes everything

 Family's grief: raw, incomplete, disbelief

 

================================================================================

THE SPECIFICITY RULE

================================================================================

 

Mandatory specifics: exact dates / exact times / exact amounts (₹) /

exact forensic details (54 बार FSL test) / exact counts / IPC/BNS sections /

distances / technology (CDR / DNA / CCTV / जिंक फास्फाइड)

 

================================================================================

THE KILLER / PERPETRATOR RULE

================================================================================

 

KILLER ARC: Normal life → hidden motive → planning → crime → cover story

→ appears to succeed → one overlooked detail → pivot → arrest → systemic meaning

 

KILLER DECEPTION TYPES:

 Type 1: Crying victim act (Priyanka — roped herself, cried on camera)

 Type 2: Cooperative informant (filed missing report himself)

 Type 3: Digital ghost (sent fake messages from victim's phone)

 Type 4: Substitute body (brought another man as "husband")

 Type 5: False backstory victim (played the trapped daughter)

 

================================================================================

THE EMOTIONAL ARCHITECTURE — 8 BEATS IN ORDER

================================================================================

 

 1. SHOCK     — Cold open. Ordinary turned sinister.

 2. CURIOSITY — Who is this? What led here?

 3. EMPATHY   — Care about the victim. Their life. Their dream.

 4. DREAD     — The plan might not be discovered. Killer is too smart.

 5. TENSION   — Investigation closing in. One wrong piece and it fails.

 6. CATHARSIS — Cover story breaks. Arrest. Confession.

 7. JUSTICE   — Named, convicted, judged. Victim acknowledged.

 8. REFLECTION — The systemic question that stays with the viewer.

 

DREAD must come BEFORE TENSION.

EMPATHY must be established before sustained DREAD.

CR-F exception: immediate controlled danger may create SHOCK/DREAD in the first

45 seconds. Establish empathy or the human stake immediately after the rewind

before continuing sustained dread and tension.

REFLECTION is NOT the CTA — it comes before CTA.

 

================================================================================

THE LOOP-BACK SYSTEM — CRIME VERSION

================================================================================

 

Write cold open AND ending together as a pair.

Cold open = unanswered question. Ending = complete answer + human cost.

 

================================================================================

REPETITION CONTROL — HARD LIMITS

================================================================================

 

Max 2 uses per script: "पुलिस के होश उड़ गए" / "पैरों तले जमीन खिसक गई" /

"कहानी ने मोड़ लिया" / "सच सामने आ गया" / "यह सिर्फ हत्या नहीं थी" /

"लेकिन" at START of paragraph (max 25% of all paragraphs)

 

================================================================================

SCENE WRITING — 2D ANIMATION READY

================================================================================

CRIME VISUAL ANCHORS REQUIRED in every investigation/evidence scene:

 CDR printout / FSL report with red stamp / fingerprint card /

 CCTV still on laptop screen / DNA match graph / deleted chat recovery /

 postmortem table / interrogation room with single light /

 locked almirah / syringe on shelf / watermelon cut open / blue drum

 

DIGITAL SCENES — present cinematically:

 Chat = bubble by bubble (not screenshot)

 Deletion = gray recovery interface

 CDR = TIME | NUMBER columns, one circled red

 Fake profile = blurred face, generic name

 

================================================================================

GORE & SENSITIVITY RULES

================================================================================

 

Violence: state the fact, not the sensation.

Discovery: describe through the finder's reaction, not the body.

Child victims: never graphic. State loss through living relatives.

Sexual violence: acknowledge as crime, do not dramatize.

Communal/religious angle: FIR facts only. Never extrapolate to communities.
