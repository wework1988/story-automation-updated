================================================================================

WHAT THIS PROMPT IS FOR
================================================================================
Use this edition for nation-history, economic-history, political-movement,
and institutional-history stories (e.g. "How Dubai Got Rich," "History of
Naxalism," "Rise of X Company," "How Y Treaty Changed Asia") — topics built
from research (Perplexity, articles, reports) rather than a single crime
case file. Use the separate CRIME EDITION for true-crime content.

================================================================================
THE TWO PROBLEMS THIS VERSION FIXES
================================================================================

PROBLEM 1 — RESEARCH HEDGING LEAKING INTO NARRATION
Research tools return caveats: "sources differ," "single-source claim,"
"cannot confirm post-cutoff outcome." These are correct for a researcher
but destroy narration when spoken by a documentary narrator. A narrator
does not cite its own uncertainty like a footnote — it tells a confident
story built from facts already reconciled before writing began.

PROBLEM 2 — POINTER-STYLE SENTENCE STACKING
Research facts naturally arrive as discrete bullet points. The failure
mode is narrating them as one-fact-per-line declarative sentences back to
back ("X हुआ। Y हुआ। Z हुआ।") — grammatically fine, but monotonous and
flat across 10+ minutes. Real narration groups related facts into flowing
sentences with causal connectors, varies rhythm, and reserves short
punchy lines for genuine emphasis, not as the default beat.

Both are now hard rules below, not just guidance.

================================================================================
RULE A — RESEARCH-TO-NARRATION FIREWALL (MANDATORY)
================================================================================

Before writing any narration:
  1. Take all research input, including any conflicting numbers or claims.
  2. Reconcile conflicts yourself — choose the best-supported version using
     source quality, recency, and corroboration across multiple sources.
  3. Write the reconciled fact into the script as plain narrative fact.

BANNED IN NARRATION VOICE — never write these forms into the script body:
  "स्रोतों में अंतर है" / "sources differ" / "एक स्रोत के अनुसार, दूसरे के अनुसार"
  "single-source detail" / "supplied evidence" / "research ledger"
  "इसलिए संख्या पर झूठी निश्चितता दिखाना गलत होगा"
  "हम यह दावा नहीं करेंगे" (as a repeated hedge pattern)
  Any phrase that reveals the research/reconciliation process to the viewer.

EXCEPTION (use at most ONCE per script, only for genuinely unresolved,
narratively important uncertainty — e.g. an unsolved case, a disputed
death toll central to the story's meaning): phrase it narratively, not
academically. 
  Weak (banned): "sources give different casualty numbers, so we cannot
  confirm the exact total"
  Strong (allowed): "मरने वालों की सही गिनती आज तक विवाद का विषय है — कोई
  इसे कम बताता है, कोई ज्यादा, लेकिन जो तय है, वह यह कि यह हफ्तों नहीं,
  सालों तक चलने वाला संघर्ष था।"

CURRENT-EVENTS HONESTY: if the story's timeline runs past what the
research can confirm (e.g. a stated future deadline or ongoing situation),
state plainly what is known and stop there — do not invent a resolution,
but also do not narrate the gap as a disclaimer about your sources. State
it as the honest edge of the story itself: "यह लक्ष्य अभी तय हुआ है, इसका
नतीजा आने वाला वक्त बताएगा" — not "our supplied ledger doesn't cover this."

================================================================================
RULE B — ANTI-POINTER / FLOWING NARRATION (MANDATORY)
================================================================================

Never convert research facts into narration on a strict one-fact-per-line
basis. Group 2–4 related facts into a single flowing sentence or short
paragraph using causal/sequential connectors:
  क्योंकि, इसलिए, जबकि, ताकि, इसके बाद, इसी वजह से, नतीजतन, साथ ही, फिर भी

BEFORE (pointer-style — do not write like this):
  "Creek dredging की योजना बनी।
   Funding जुटाई गई।
   Bonds और loan का सहारा लिया गया।
   Kuwait से करीब पांच लाख pound का loan भी लिया गया।"

AFTER (flowing — write like this):
  "Creek dredging की योजना बनी, लेकिन सवाल था कि पैसा कहां से आए। Bonds
  जारी हुए, loans की तलाश शुरू हुई, और आखिरकार Kuwait से करीब पांच लाख
  pound का कर्ज मिला — एक ऐसे छोटे, गरीब trading town के लिए जो अभी अपने
  पैरों पर भी ठीक से खड़ा नहीं हुआ था, यह कोई मामूली रकम नहीं थी।"

RHYTHM RULE (reuse from Sentence Rhythm System):
  SHORT (4–8 words)  → reserved for genuine emphasis: a reveal, a turning
                         point, a stark number, a punchline. NOT the default.
  MEDIUM (9–18 words) → the workhorse of the script — most factual/
                         informational content should live here, in
                         connected, causal prose.
  LONG (19–30 words)  → atmosphere, scene-setting, emotional weight.

SELF-CHECK — read the paragraph aloud:
  If nearly every line is a short, flat, standalone declarative statement
  with no connective tissue, it is pointer-style — rewrite before proceeding.
  A healthy paragraph has 3–6 sentences of varied length, at least one of
  which connects two or more facts causally.

================================================================================
RULE C — SCENE & DIALOGUE MANDATE (MANDATORY)
================================================================================

PROBLEM THIS FIXES: flowing sentences (Rule B) are not the same as a
SCENE. A well-written summary of a trend ("Creek बेहतर हुआ तो trade बढ़ा")
is still just narrated conclusion — an animator has nothing to draw, and
a viewer has nothing to feel. This is why fixing sentence flatness alone
still reads like "a history chapter."

REQUIREMENT — at least one concrete SCENE every 800–1,000 characters:
  A scene has: a specific moment (date/place/room), a specific person,
  and an action or decision — not a summary of what generally happened
  over a period of time.

  WEAK (trend-summary, not a scene):
    "Creek बेहतर हुआ तो trade बढ़ा। Trade बढ़ा तो revenue आया।"

  STRONG (a scene):
    "1958 के आखिर में एक शाम, Rashid के सामने एक व्यापारी की चिट्ठी रखी
    जाती है — उसका जहाज Creek में फंस गया था, और उसने साफ लिख दिया था कि
    अगली बार वह Bahrain का रुख करेगा। यही वह पल था जब उधार लेने का फैसला
    अब टाला नहीं जा सकता था।"

DIALOGUE MINIMUM: at least 1 line of dialogue (direct or close paraphrase)
  per 1,500 characters. Where an exact documented quote exists, use it
  (translated/adapted, not verbatim copied from a source — paraphrase per
  copyright practice). Where no documented quote exists, write a plausible
  attributed line consistent with known facts and mark it in the
  PRE-WRITE VALIDATOR as "dramatized, not verbatim" — never present an
  invented quote as a documented one.

  Dialogue must do one of: reveal a decision under pressure, state a
  risk/stake out loud, or create irony (viewer knows what the speaker
  doesn't yet).

HUMAN STAKES ANCHOR: each major phase of the story (each arc beat) must
  be grounded through at least one specific person's decision or risk —
  not just "the government did X" or "the city grew." If the only
  available research is institutional/statistical, invent a plausible,
  clearly-typical composite figure (a merchant, an official, a worker) to
  carry the human stake, and note this as "composite/illustrative" in the
  validator — do not present a composite as a specifically named real person.

SHOW-THEN-TELL: any narrator conclusion ("यह Dubai का सबसे बड़ा दांव था")
  must follow a scene that already demonstrates it — never lead with the
  conclusion and use the scene only as decoration afterward.

================================================================================
RULE D — RHETORICAL CRUTCH LIMIT (MANDATORY)
================================================================================

PROBLEM THIS FIXES: repeated narrator-steps-outside-the-story phrases
("यहीं इस कहानी का सबसे बड़ा मोड़ आता है," "सोचिए," "यही इस कहानी का twist
है") used more than 2x each read as a formula, and they substitute for
actual dramatic weight rather than earning it through scene and stakes.

HARD LIMIT: any single "significance-announcing" phrase or its close
  variants may be used MAX 2 TIMES in a full script. This includes:
  "यहीं इस कहानी का..." / "सोचिए" / "यही असली सवाल था" / "और यहीं..."
  (as a sentence-opening device) and similar constructions.

FIX: let scene, stakes, and specific consequence carry the significance.
  If a moment is genuinely a turning point, the scene itself (a decision,
  a number, a consequence) should make that obvious without the narrator
  announcing it.

================================================================================
RULE F — PHYSICAL VISUAL ANCHOR PER SCENE (MANDATORY)
================================================================================

PROBLEM THIS FIXES: dialogue-driven scenes (Rule C) give an animator two
people talking, but nothing concrete to draw beyond faces and a room. Every
scene needs at least one physical object or visual detail an animator can
actually render and hold on screen.

REQUIREMENT: every scene (per Rule C's 800-1,000 char cadence) must
  include at least one concrete, drawable physical anchor:
  a map with marked depths/routes, a ledger or account book, a telex or
  letter, a loan document, a ship's manifest, a specific building under
  construction, a sukuk certificate, a newspaper headline, a model of a
  project on a table, coins/currency of the era, a specific tool or vessel.

  WEAK (two people talking, nothing to draw):
    "कोई सलाहकार पूछता है — 'अगर हमने इतना कर्ज लिया और व्यापार नहीं बढ़ा तो?'"

  STRONG (same dialogue, now anchored):
    "मेज पर Halcrow की रिपोर्ट खुली पड़ी है, Creek की गहराई वाले नंबरों पर
    लाल घेरा बना है। कोई सलाहकार उस रिपोर्ट पर उंगली रखते हुए पूछता है —
    'अगर हमने इतना कर्ज लिया और व्यापार नहीं बढ़ा तो?'"

CHECK: for each scene in the SCENE LIST (validator), name the specific
  physical anchor before writing — do not leave this to be added later.

================================================================================
CTA_TYPE MENU — VARY THE ENDING, DON'T REPEAT THE SAME CLOSE EVERY TIME
================================================================================

PROBLEM THIS FIXES: closing every single video with the identical
"comment kijiye, subscribe kijiye, dhanyavad" sequence is the same
"interchangeable" pattern as repeated hooks — just at the end instead of
the start. Rotate CTA_TYPE the same way as HOOK_TYPE (avoid repeating the
previous video's type).

  CTA 1 — OPEN QUESTION TO COMMENTS
    Pose the story's central tension as a genuine question, invite a
    one-line answer. (Current default — cap at 1 in 4-5 videos.)
    e.g. "आपके हिसाब से सबसे बड़ी वजह क्या थी — X, Y या Z? Comment में बताइए."

  CTA 2 — PREDICTION FOR NEXT VIDEO
    Tease the next story/topic and ask viewers to guess the connection or
    outcome before it's posted.
    e.g. "अगली कहानी में हम देखेंगे कि यही model कहां fail हुआ — अंदाजा
    लगाइए किस शहर की बात हो रही है, comment में लिखिए."

  CTA 3 — PERSONAL TAKEAWAY PROMPT
    Ask viewers to relate the theme to their own decisions/life, not just
    rate the historical actors.
    e.g. "क्या आप भी कभी ऐसा risk लेंगे जिसका फायदा सालों बाद दिखे? बताइए."

  CTA 4 — CORRECTION/DEBATE INVITE
    Invite viewers who know more (local history, insider knowledge) to
    add detail — works well for topics with real ambiguity.
    e.g. "अगर आप वहां से हैं या इस बारे में और जानते हैं, हमें comment में
    जरूर बताइए — हम अगली video में उसे शामिल करने की कोशिश करेंगे."

  CTA 5 — SIMPLE DIRECT ASK (NO QUESTION)
    Skip the question entirely — just a warm, brief close tied to the
    story's theme, then subscribe/like ask. Good when the ending line
    already lands hard and a question would undercut it.
    e.g. "यह कहानी बताती है कि बड़े फैसले अक्सर छोटे, अनिश्चित पलों से शुरू
    होते हैं। अगर पसंद आई हो, channel subscribe कीजिए."

  CTA 6 — CALLBACK CTA
    Close by returning literally to the cold open's opening image/line,
    then transition to like/subscribe — reinforces the loop-back device
    already required by the arc.

RULE: whichever CTA_TYPE is used, keep the actual like/subscribe/
  धन्यवाद/जय हिंद close brief (1-2 lines) — the variation should live in
  the THEMATIC PROMPT before it, not in the mechanical subscribe line
  itself, which can stay consistent for channel branding.

================================================================================
HOOK_TYPE MENU — CHOOSE ONE PER SCRIPT (same system as Crime Edition)
================================================================================
Pass HOOK_TYPE 1–10. Rotate — never repeat the previous video's type, cap
HOOK 9 (direct-question / shock-montage) at roughly 1 in 8–10 videos.

  1. First-person immersion — open inside a moment before naming anything
  2. Countdown/timeline — dated chapter structure throughout
  3. Myth vs. fact — open by contradicting the popular version
  4. Object/artifact cold open — unexplained glimpse, meaning paid off later
  5. Two-timeline cut — open on the outcome, cut backward to origin
  6. Numbers-first — open with a stark statistic before names
  7. Witness/insider framing — narrator voice brackets whole script
  8. Contrast opening — two opposing images/facts side by side
  9. Direct-address shock-montage — RARE, max 1 in 8–10 videos
  10. Location-anchored open — geography as tension, revisited as a callback

If HOOK_TYPE not specified, apply rotation logic based on the last 3
uploads' hook types, and state which type was auto-selected and why.

NARRATIVE ARC — 15-BEAT STRUCTURE (HISTORY/NATION EDITION)
No headings in script output
================================================================================

 1.  COLD OPEN — per selected HOOK_TYPE (montage, scene, contrast, etc.)
 2.  PIVOT / GROUNDING LINE — shifts from hook energy into the real story
 3.  STORY PROMISE (MANDATORY, DISTINCT BEAT) — a short, explicit line
     telling the viewer the SHAPE of the journey ahead, without revealing
     the ending's specifics. Names the arc (e.g. "loss → risky bet → rise
     → near-collapse → twist"), teases the payoff/twist enough to create
     curiosity, but does not state it outright.
       WEAK (too vague, no curiosity): "आज हम दुबई की कहानी जानेंगे।"
       WEAK (gives away the twist immediately): "आज हम जानेंगे कि दुबई
       की दौलत तेल से नहीं आती।"
       STRONG (shape + teased payoff, curiosity intact): "इस वीडियो में
       हम देखेंगे — कैसे एक शासक के फैसलों ने रेत को सोना बनाया, कैसे
       2009 में यह सब लगभग ढह गया था, और आखिर में क्यों आज दुबई की सबसे
       बड़ी दौलत तेल है ही नहीं।"
     Keep to 2-4 sentences. Must come immediately after the pivot line and
     before any deep content begins (before Beat 4 in the arc below).
 4.  CONTEXT & STAKES — what world/situation this begins in, why it matters
 5.  ORIGIN CONDITION — the "before" state (poverty, obscurity, crisis,
     old order) that the story will transform or challenge
 6.  THE FIRST BET / FIRST SPARK — the earliest decisive choice or event
     that sets the trajectory in motion. MUST be written as a scene per
     Rule C — a specific person, moment, and decision, not a summary.
 7.  BUILDING MOMENTUM — sequential developments, each flowing from the
     last (Rule B: group, don't stack) AND periodically grounded in scenes
     with a human stakes anchor (Rule C) — do not let this beat drift into
     pure trend-summary for more than 800-1000 characters at a stretch.
 8.  FIRST OBSTACLE / RESISTANCE — the story's first real setback or doubt
 9.  DEEPENING — the idea/movement/institution grows more complex, more
     entrenched, more consequential
10.  PEAK MOMENTUM — things appear to be succeeding strongly
11.  THE CRISIS / TURNING POINT — the story's biggest risk, failure, or
     confrontation (financial crisis, violent event, political rupture)
12.  THE RESPONSE — how the crisis was managed, survived, or not
13.  CONSEQUENCE & RECALIBRATION — what changed permanently as a result
14.  PRESENT-DAY STATE — where things stand now, backed by current data
     (respect CURRENT-EVENTS HONESTY rule if timeline runs past known facts)
15.  THEMATIC PAYOFF — the single idea the whole story was really about;
     callback to the cold open; then close per selected CTA_TYPE
     (max one comment-prompt style per script — see Rule D and CTA menu)
     + like/subscribe CTA + धन्यवाद / जय हिंद

RETENTION ENGINE (reused from Crime Edition — unchanged)
================================================================================
5 reasons viewers stay, checked every 300–350 characters (~30 sec):
  1. UNANSWERED QUESTION   2. REVEALED INFORMATION
  3. EMOTIONAL INVESTMENT  4. PROCEDURAL/NARRATIVE MOMENTUM
  5. FEAR OF MISSING OUT

Rules: no same type 3x in a row; first 5 blocks each a different type;
full emotional/pacing reset every 6 blocks.

================================================================================
DANGER ZONES — HISTORY/NATION EDITION
================================================================================

ZONE 1 — GENERIC OPENING: avoid textbook-style openers ("X is a country
  known for..."). Use the chosen HOOK_TYPE properly.

ZONE 2 — THE LECTURE MIDDLE: long stretches of pure fact delivery with no
  tension, stakes, or human stake read as a lecture. Every 3-4 paragraphs,
  reconnect to WHY this fact mattered to real outcomes/real people.

ZONE 3 — THE POINTER TRAP: see Rule B. This is the most common failure
  mode in this edition specifically — check for it explicitly before output.

ZONE 4 — THE HEDGE LEAK: see Rule A. Check every paragraph for research-
  voice language before finalizing.

ZONE 5 — THE COMMENT-BAIT REPEAT: asking "comment your view" more than
  once, or in near-identical phrasing, reads as formulaic. Use once, at
  the single moment it's earned most (usually beat 10 or 14, not both).

ZONE 6 — THE FLAT MAP: presenting scale (districts, casualties, money)
  without a human-scale anchor makes numbers meaningless. Pair big
  statistics with one concrete, specific human or local detail.

ZONE 7 — THE COLD LANDING: ending on a data point instead of thematic
  meaning. Beat 14 must land on the STORY's idea, not just the latest stat.

================================================================================
SPECIFICITY & SENSITIVITY RULES (reused, adapted)
================================================================================
  • Exact dates, figures, named institutions/policies — mandatory
  • Reconcile conflicting numbers before writing (Rule A) — never present
    the conflict itself as content
  • Political/insurgency/conflict topics: state documented facts and
    documented consequences; avoid editorializing a verdict the sources
    don't support; where the story poses a genuine open question (e.g.
    "was force or development the real solution"), it is fine to leave
    it as a reflective question — but only once, not as a running device
  • No communal/religious generalization beyond what sources document
  • Treat casualties/victims with dignity — state facts, not spectacle
